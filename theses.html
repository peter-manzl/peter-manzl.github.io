PhD thesis

2024Dr. techn.Grade 1.0

Multibody Dynamics Towards Artificial Intelligence and Robotics

Supervisor: Univ.-Prof. Johannes Gerstmayr (Innsbruck). Second reviewer: Prof. Dan Negrut (University of Wisconsin-Madison). Submitted 31 October 2024. Awarded the Lagrange Award and the Hypo Tirol Dissertation Award.

This cumulative thesis connects multibody dynamics with machine learning to make simulation more efficient in robotics and engineering. It starts with LeoBot, a Mecanum-wheeled mobile manipulator, and new, experimentally validated Mecanum wheel models. It continues with the "comfort zone" base-placement strategy for mobile manipulators, and a reliability study of reinforcement learning on systems of increasing complexity. Reinforcement learning also stabilizes and steers an intelligent bicycle. Large language models then create simulation models from natural language, and the thesis ends with SLIDE, a method that estimates the dynamic response of multibody systems with large speedups.

Master's thesis

2020Dipl.-Ing.Grade 1.0

Realtime Movement of a Mecanum Wheeled Robot using the Robot Operating System ROS

Supervisor: Univ.-Prof. Johannes Gerstmayr. Submitted 2 September 2020.

The thesis covers the control software for the mobile manipulator LeoBot, built for the RoboCup@Work league: a real-time Linux kernel (PREEMPT_RT) with ROS, EtherCAT communication to Maxon EPOS-4 motor controllers, and simulation of the Mecanum wheels. The wheel models grew into the publications on LeoBot and Mecanum wheel friction.

Bachelor's thesis

2018BSc

Development of a serial 6R robot

Design and control of a serial robot with six rotary joints and a spherical wrist, using MATLAB/Simulink and microcontrollers. It includes CAN-bus communication, kinematics, collision avoidance and path planning, and 3D-printed breakaway links for safety. The robot was developed further during my time as student assistant (2018–2020).
