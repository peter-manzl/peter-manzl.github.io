Research

My group builds robot-based systems for quality assurance and manufacturing and combines physics-based simulation with machine learning. Application areas include aerospace, automotive and energy technology.

Robotics group, Augsburg

The AG Robotik is part of Mechanical Engineering at the Institute for Materials Resource Management. The goal is adaptive, robot-supported systems that give reproducible, precise and efficient processes.

Collision avoidance & path planning

Motion planning adapted to each object and optimized for reachability and pose-dependent loads, to shorten or enable cycle times.

Robot-based component testing

Force-moment data is used actively in control, to compensate unwanted load components or to hold a trajectory on a target load vector.

Robot-based inspection

Several cooperating robots carry out radiography, computed tomography and optical inspection of large objects. The focus is on inspection sequences and sensor fusion.

Robot-based bending

Force- and moment-controlled two-arm systems produce complex structures without extra joining steps, combined with AI models of the process.

Friction stir welding

Sensors and AI monitor and optimize robotic friction stir welding in real time, to reduce scrap and add quality assurance.

Sim2Real

Simulation models are transferred to real robot applications for planning, optimization and virtual commissioning, and measurement data flows back into the models.

Current projects

2026–2028CFP Augsburg

RAISE – Robotic AI System for Inspection and Evaluation

An autonomous robotic inspection cell that selects and runs suitable non-destructive tests for each part, with human-in-the-loop control. Partners: Airbus, Porsche, BMW.

2026–2028CFP Augsburg

KIBiS – AI-based control of chained robotic bending and injection-molding joining

An AI-controlled process chain that combines robotic profile bending with injection-molded joining, using fast surrogate models and sensor data. Partners: ANYBRID, KUKA, IPR.

2023–2026BayVFP

KoKIRo – Cognitive, AI-controlled robot assembly system

A flexible cobot assembly system with a digital twin, validated on tasks such as peg-in-hole assembly and screw handling. Partners: Fraunhofer IGCV, pro-micron, robominds, azero.

Earlier research topics

Machine learning for multibody systems

SLIDE, a learning-based method that estimates the forced dynamic response of multibody systems in real time, including flexible structures in hydraulically actuated machines. Also surrogate models for compliant joints and reinforcement learning for mechanical systems.

See publications →

Simulation models from language

Large language models that create, evaluate and self-validate multibody simulation models from natural-language descriptions.

Mecanum wheels & mobile manipulators

A Mecanum wheel model based on orthotropic friction with experimental validation, the base-placement strategy "Task-Dependent Comfort Zone", and the LeoBot platform.

Exudyn

Open-source Python/C++ software for the simulation of flexible multibody systems. I contributed code for (mobile) robotics and the machine-learning modules. github.com/jgerstmayr/EXUDYN

RoboCup@Work, Innsbruck

Development and research of mobile manipulators for industrial applications, 2018–2023. LeoBot repository

Interdisciplinary robotics

Since 2025: a robotic prototype developed with research groups in pharmaceutical technology and stroke research.
