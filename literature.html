2026

  • Path Following and Stabilization of a Bicycle Model using a Reinforcement Learning Approach
    S. Weyrer, P. Manzl, A. L. Schwab, et al. · Multibody System Dynamics 67, pp. 775–810 · 10.1007/s11044-026-10144-x

    The bicycle is an interesting example for an unstable multibody system where the instability depneds on the forward velocity. While most work is investigating either path following or stabilization we show that a single agent is able to learn both tasks simultaniously with great performance. Using methods of explainable AI (SHAP values) we were able to show that the model learns the analtically known principle of countersteering.

  • SLIDE: A machine-learning based method for forced dynamic response estimation of multibody systems
    P. Manzl, A. Humer, Q. Khadim, J. Gerstmayr · Mechanics Based Design of Structures and Machines, pp. 1–29 · 10.1080/15397734.2025.2559325

    We investigated machine learning methods to train surrogate models. These surrogate models reflect the dynamics of a given system and can replace classical dynamical solvers with increadible speedups. In the case of damped systems with forced excitation, the damping can be exploited to eliminate the otherwise required initial conditions of the systems and instead using a longer input window in connection with a truncated output window of the system.

  • Creation, evaluation and self-validation of simulation models with large language models
    T. Möltner, P. Manzl, M. Pieber, J. Gerstmayr · Neurocomputing, 132030 · 10.1016/j.neucom.2025.132030

    Large Language Models (LLMs) show strong problem solving and coding capabilities. In the domain of mechanical engineering and multibody dynamics they can be used to create simulation models. In this publication we investigated open-source LLMs - which can run on local machines, eliminating the widely discussed issue of data autonomy, and how they can be used to create and evaluate simulation models. Furthermore an approach of self-validation through other LLMs is explored and evaluated.

  • Surrogate Models for Compliant Joints in Programmable Structures
    M. Pieber, Z. Zhang, P. Manzl, J. Gerstmayr · Journal of Computational and Nonlinear Dynamics 20(8), 081010 · 10.1115/1.4068579

    Complient mechanisms show great promise for miniturizing joints in adaptive robot cells which can attach, dettach and reconfigure themselfes. One problem of using complient mechanisms for joints is the inevitably occuring parasitic motion making them non-ideal joints. In this work we investigated surrogate models (using polinomials and neural networks) and an approach how these surrogate models can be used in simulation to minimize the task-space error occuring in robotics applications.

  • Multibody Models Generated from Natural Language
    J. Gerstmayr, P. Manzl, M. Pieber · Multibody System Dynamics 62, pp. 249–271 · 10.1007/s11044-023-09962-0

    In beginning of 2023 when we started the first experiments it was for many experts unthinkable that Large Language Models (LLMs) would be able to create simulation models just on a basis of natural language descriptions. In this paper we explore approaches and the performance of early LLMs like GPT-3.5, Bard, Llama-2 and (at that time newest and strongest model) GPT-4. Looking at the results a few years later the quatnum leap LLMs performed is very appearant as the performance of a small open-weight LLM in 2026 outperforms all models of 2024.

  • A Mecanum wheel model based on orthotropic friction with experimental validation
    P. Manzl, M. Sereinig, J. Gerstmayr · Mechanism and Machine Theory 193, 105548 · 10.1016/j.mechmachtheory.2023.105548

    The Mechanum wheel is an very interesting type of wheel with rollers on it's circomference. Most research on Mecanum wheeled robots is aknowledging the non-ideal effects and investigating sophisticated control approaches how to compensate the effects. In this research we did a large-scale investigation on non-ideal effects and influences like changing COM and different velocities. In addition to that we show a very efficient simulation model with is faster than realtime, enabling the simulation model to be used in closed-loop control approaches.

  • Task-Dependent Comfort Zone, a Base Placement Strategy for Mobile Manipulators Based on Manipulability Measures
    M. Sereinig, P. Manzl, J. Gerstmayr · Robotics 13(8) · 10.3390/robotics13080122

    Mobile manipulators typically are redundant systems where a for the manipulation of an object the manipulator can place it's platform relative to an object in a wide area. To optimize the manipulation of the object, we propse the conecpt of the _comfort zone_ - an approach to evaluate how well a task can be performed by the robot in a given pose (relative to the object).

  • Reliability evaluation of reinforcement learning methods for mechanical systems with increasing complexity
    P. Manzl, O. Rogov, J. Gerstmayr, A. Mikkola, G. Orzechowski · Multibody System Dynamics, special issue on Artificial Intelligence within Multibody System Dynamics, pp. 1–25 · 10.1007/s11044-023-09960-2

    Reinforcement Learning (RL) is well known as a powerful tool of learning a policy - basically an agent's strategy on how to choose a given action depending on the observed environment - and is often applied to control problems. A major issue of applying RL to real control problems is the lack of safety guarantees. In this work we investigate how reliable an agent can control the N-link cartpole for N ∈{1,2,3} and visualizing the failure areas of given agents. We also find blind spots in agents with discrete action-space.
